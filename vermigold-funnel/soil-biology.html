The ecosystem under your feet

A single teaspoon of healthy soil holds billions of bacteria, miles of fungal thread, and a food web that quietly trades nutrients for sugar all season long. Worm castings, beneficial bacteria, fungi, and insect frass aren't additives — they're the workforce that turns dead ground into a living one.

Why fertilizer alone doesn't fix it

Fertilizer feeds the plant for a week. Biology feeds the plant — and the soil, and next year's soil — for good. Compaction, crusting, and nutrient lockout are symptoms of one root problem: there isn't enough life down there to run the system.

What rebuilding actually looks like

Reintroduce the biology, protect it with cover and no-till habits, and keep it fed and hydrated through the year — and the soil starts doing the work itself. That's the whole system this almanac walks through, month by month.
